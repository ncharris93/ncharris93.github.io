title: I don't give a damn about UX*
---

\*This statement is both true and false.

The UX I care about isn’t the one most people think of.<br/>
The users I care about are probably not the users you're thinking of.<br/>
My UX is better known as DX: Developer Experience.

As engineers, we architect and build systems. But most of the time, we’re not starting from scratch. We’re assembling, shaping, and connecting pieces that other engineers created. We stack complex technologies to solve complex problems, and it can quickly get out of hand.

That’s why the quality of our tools matters. A lot.<br/>
How intuitive they are.<br/>
How composable.<br/>
How much mental friction they introduce.

I care about tools that respect the developer’s mind - their attention, their time, their cognitive overhead.

Good DX starts with clean interfaces and ends with trust.<br/>
It’s not about being clever. It’s about being clear.<br/>
And honestly, it’s hard to stress that too much.

My favorite modern example: TanStack Query. With the introduction of React hooks, it took something messy and repetitive and made it elegant. Before, we had boilerplate. Now, we have a simple, focused interface that does one job extremely well.

Or go back to the roots: Unix tools like `awk`, `sed`, and `grep`. Each one solves a single problem. But when you combine them, they become powerful.<br/>
That’s the magic of composability.<br/>
That’s DX done right.<br/>

To me, great Developer Experience is a form of respect.  <br/>
It shows up in the details.<br/>
It creates leverage.<br/>
It protects time, focus, and flow.<br/>
Great DX is invisible - and that’s what makes it beautiful.

I think I’ve always been wired this way.

Back in 4th grade, we were working on a holiday craft project. One step involved threading yarn through dozens of eyelets. Most kids started by hand. But not me. I paused, grabbed a paperclip, bent it into a needle, and then got to work. I started after everyone else, but I finished faster.  <br/>
It wasn’t about being smarter.  <br/>
It was about building the right tool.

That mindset never left.

> Often the best thing you can do is “Work smarter, not harder.”<br/>
>
> — my old hockey coach, Mr. Peet.

DX is culture.  <br/>
It’s philosophy.  <br/>
It’s care, baked into the system.

So no, I don’t give a damn about UX - unless you mean the kind that shows up in the terminal, in the code editor, or in the mental models we build and share.

In that case? <br/>
I care a lot.

And if you work on a team, I think you should care too.
